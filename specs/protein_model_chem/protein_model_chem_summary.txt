1. The core problem or question
Central problem
The project aims to answer:

“How do mutations perturb the structural interaction and energetic constraint network underlying a protein’s shape, and what mechanistic structural consequences may result?”

The system is intended to:


analyze protein structures from AlphaFold (AF) and PDBe,


detect chemically meaningful interaction networks,


estimate interaction/constraint perturbations caused by mutations,


and explain how those perturbations may alter local or global structural compatibility.


The core output is not merely structural visualization, but:

interpretable mechanistic reasoning grounded in structural chemistry.


Why this problem matters
Structural biologists routinely inspect:


AlphaFold models,


experimental structures,


interaction networks,


and mutations,


but most workflows remain:


manual,


fragmented,


visualization-heavy,


and weakly interpretable.


Current tools often:


show contacts without explaining significance,


provide opaque prediction scores,


or overclaim mechanistic certainty.


This project addresses the gap between:


static structure inspection,
and


mechanistic structural interpretation.



Who it matters to
Primary users:


structural biologists


protein scientists


translational researchers


mutation-focused experimentalists


Users are assumed to understand:


protein chemistry


folding


structural biology


Users are not assumed to:


code,


interpret raw force-field outputs,


or manually derive interaction-network reasoning.



What capability or insight this enables
The system enables users to:


understand why a structure adopts a given shape,


identify structurally important residues,


analyze mutation-induced interaction perturbations,


reason about compatibility/incompatibility with the original fold,


and generate mechanistic hypotheses about structural consequences.


Key insight:

mutations should be understood as redistributions of structural constraints, not simple residue substitutions.


Why this approach is meaningful
The project occupies a scientifically valuable niche between:


structure viewers,


mutation predictors,


and black-box pathogenicity systems.


The product’s differentiator is:

interpretable structural reasoning.

Rather than predicting “truth” (e.g. pathogenicity), the system:


explains structural consequences,


visualizes interaction-network perturbations,


and highlights compatibility shifts in the structural constraint landscape.


This makes the system:


scientifically defensible,


educationally valuable,


and mechanistically interpretable.



2. How the system/product works
High-level workflow
The system:


retrieves structures from AF and/or PDBe,


normalizes and parses structures,


computes interaction networks and energetic approximations,


models mutation-induced perturbations,


compares WT vs mutant interaction topology,


generates mechanistic interpretations,


ranks structural disruption likelihood.



Core operational logic
The system models proteins as:

structural constraint networks.

A protein fold is treated as:


a network of interacting residues,


stabilized through cooperative energetic compatibility.


Mutations are analyzed as:

perturbations to that compatibility network.

The system reasons about:


interaction loss,


steric incompatibility,


packing disruption,


centrality loss,


and altered constraint density.



Inputs
Primary inputs


AlphaFold structures


PDBe experimental structures


Optional inputs


mutation(s)


chain selection


residue selection



Structural provenance policy
Decision:


support AF and PDBe equally as input sources,


but treat PDBe structures as higher-confidence evidence when available.


Default behavior:


prefer PDBe if high-quality experimental structure exists,


otherwise use AF.


Manual override should remain available.

Structural interaction layer
The system detects:


hydrogen bonds


salt bridges


hydrophobic contacts


aromatic interactions


disulfides


steric clashes


local packing contacts


These interactions form:

residue-level interaction graphs.


Energetic reasoning layer
The system uses:


heuristic/empirical energetic estimates,
NOT:


full thermodynamic free-energy calculations.


Important implementation decision:


avoid claiming exact physical energies or true ΔΔG.


The system estimates:


interaction favorability,


compatibility shifts,


local destabilization signals.


The product should avoid:


total stability scores,


deterministic energetic claims.



Network analysis layer
The system models:


residues as graph nodes,


interactions as edges.


Edge weights may incorporate:


interaction type,


interaction confidence,


approximate energetic importance.


Network-derived signals include:


interaction density


centrality


network fragmentation


local connectivity loss


constraint redundancy


The network model is intended to represent:

structural constraint architecture.


Mutation analysis workflow
Mutation analysis focuses on:


local compatibility disruption,


not exact structural prediction.


Mutation workflow:


apply residue substitution,


recompute local interaction environment,


estimate perturbation to interaction network,


identify constraint incompatibilities,


infer likely structural adaptation pressures.



Mutation consequence categories
The system explicitly supports interpretation of:
Secondary structure disruption
Strongest and most defensible category.
Examples:


proline insertion,


glycine flexibility,


β-sheet incompatibility,


helix packing disruption.



Interface disruption
Moderately strong.
Examples:


loss of interface packing,


disrupted electrostatics,


weakened buried contacts.



Flexibility changes
Interpret cautiously.
The system does NOT directly predict dynamics.
Instead it infers:


reduced packing,


weaker constraints,


increased local adaptability signals.



Structural destabilization signals
Preferred terminology over “unfolding risk.”
The system identifies:


destabilizing structural features,
NOT:


actual folding thermodynamics.



Core conceptual framework
The system operates through:
Compatibility analysis
Does the mutation fit the original structural environment?

Constraint redistribution
How does the mutation alter stabilizing interactions?

Interaction-network perturbation
How does the mutation affect local cooperative stabilization?

Structural adaptation pressure
What regions may require conformational accommodation?

Output model
Outputs are layered into:
Observation
Directly computed facts.
Examples:


lost hydrogen bond,


cavity introduced,


clash detected.



Interpretation
Mechanistic reasoning.
Examples:


disrupted aromatic packing,


weakened hydrophobic core.



Hypothesis
Higher-level speculative consequence.
Examples:


local flexibility increase possible,


local destabilization plausible.



Severity ranking
Decision:


rank structural disruption likelihood,
NOT:


pathogenicity or disease severity.


Severity derives from:


structural interactions


energetic estimates


network centrality


NOT:


evolutionary conservation


clinical databases


genomics evidence



Key assumptions
The system assumes:


static structures are informative,


local interactions approximate stabilizing constraints,


interaction-network perturbation correlates with structural sensitivity.



Known weaknesses
The system does not model:


full thermodynamics,


ensemble dynamics,


entropy,


solvent explicitly,


kinetics,


allostery,


folding pathways,


true conformational landscapes.



3. Constraints, caveats, and reliability considerations
Structural biology limitations
Static structures are not full biological reality
Why it matters:
Proteins are dynamic ensembles, not rigid objects.
Risk:
Users may overinterpret static interactions as deterministic.
Communication requirement:
The UI should explicitly communicate:


“structural hypothesis”


“interaction compatibility”


“possible structural adaptation”
instead of deterministic claims.


Mitigation:
Use layered confidence and inference categories.

AlphaFold-specific limitations
AF confidence does not equal biological certainty
AF is strong for:


local geometry,


globular folds.


AF is weaker for:


interfaces,


disorder,


conformational heterogeneity,


ligand-induced states,


alternative assemblies.


Risk:
False mechanistic narratives from uncertain coordinates.
Mitigation:


propagate pLDDT into interpretation confidence,


downweight low-confidence regions,


visually mark uncertain interactions.



Experimental structure limitations
PDBe structures are not absolute truth.
Potential issues:


crystal packing artifacts,


missing residues,


low-resolution sidechains,


engineered constructs,


incomplete assemblies.


Communication:
Experimental provenance should improve confidence but not imply certainty.

Energetic limitations
Energies are heuristic
The system does not compute:


rigorous free energies,


validated ΔΔG values,


molecular dynamics ensembles.


Risk:
False precision.
Mitigation:


avoid exact energetic claims,


prefer qualitative destabilization reasoning,


avoid authoritative numeric outputs.



Network model limitations
Graph metrics depend entirely on:


interaction definitions,


weighting assumptions,


geometric thresholds.


Risk:
Artificially convincing but biologically meaningless centrality rankings.
Mitigation:


ensure interpretability,


expose interaction definitions,


avoid overclaiming network significance.



Mutation interpretation limitations
The system cannot reliably predict:


exact mutant conformations,


actual unfolding,


true flexibility changes,


allosteric propagation,


biological pathogenicity.


Communication:
Use language such as:


“consistent with”


“may require”


“suggests”


“possible destabilization”


Avoid:


“causes”


“abolishes”


“guarantees”



Missing biological context
V1 excludes:


ligands


cofactors


PTMs


nucleic acids


membrane energetics


Risk:
Incorrect mechanistic interpretation in ligand- or environment-dependent proteins.
Mitigation:
Warn users when context may be missing.

Structural normalization challenges
Implementation risks:


residue numbering mismatches,


chain naming inconsistencies,


alternate conformations,


missing loops,


biological assembly ambiguity.


Mitigation:
Implement robust structure normalization layer.

Applicability limits
The system should be cautious for:


disordered proteins,


membrane proteins,


multimer uncertainty,


metal-dependent proteins,


flexible loops,


low-confidence AF regions.



Trust architecture
The system should distinguish:
TypeMeaningStructural confidencetrust in coordinatesInteraction confidencetrust in interaction geometryMechanistic confidencetrust in interpretation
This distinction is foundational.

4. Scope decisions
Input sources
Decision:
Support both AF and PDBe structures.
Reason:
Both are valuable structural hypotheses.
Tradeoff:
Need provenance-aware confidence handling.

Experimental preference
Decision:
Prefer PDBe structures when good experimental data exists.
Reason:
Higher structural reliability.
Tradeoff:
Experimental structures may be incomplete.

Product positioning
Decision:
Build an interpretable structural reasoning system.
NOT:


pathogenicity predictor,


clinical classifier,


full stability engine.


Reason:
Maintains scientific defensibility and interpretability.

Evidence model
Decision:
Use only:


structural interactions,


energetic estimates,


network centrality.


Do NOT include:


conservation,


genomics,


clinical evidence,


evolutionary scores.


Reason:
Avoid mixing incompatible evidence modalities.
Tradeoff:
Reduced predictive breadth.

Interaction granularity
Decision:
Operate primarily at:


residue level


interaction-network level.


Reason:
Best balance between interpretability and detail.

Mutation modeling
Decision:
Focus on:


compatibility perturbation,


interaction redistribution,


local structural adaptation pressure.


NOT:


exact mutant structure prediction.


Reason:
Scientifically safer and more explainable.

Visualization strategy
Decision:
Prioritize:


interaction perturbation maps,


network changes,


compatibility shifts.


NOT:


authoritative mutant structure simulations.


Reason:
Avoid false geometric certainty.

Severity scoring
Decision:
Rank:


structural disruption likelihood.


NOT:


disease severity,


pathogenicity,


clinical outcome.


Reason:
System lacks biological-context evidence.

Supported mechanistic categories
Decision:
Support:


secondary structure disruption,


interface disruption,


flexibility-related constraint changes,


structural destabilization signals.


Reason:
Scientifically interpretable from static structures.

Confidence handling
Decision:
Confidence must propagate from:


structure source,


local confidence,


interaction robustness.


Reason:
Prevent overinterpretation.

V1 exclusions
Explicitly out of scope:


MD simulations,


folding free-energy prediction,


ligand energetics,


allostery,


membrane energetics,


ensemble thermodynamics,


exact conformational prediction,


clinical pathogenicity prediction.


Reason:
Too speculative and computationally complex for defensible MVP.

5. User journey / narrative flow
Step 1 — Load structure
User:


selects AF or PDBe structure.


System:


retrieves structure,


normalizes chains/residues,


annotates provenance/confidence.


User sees:


structure visualization,


confidence indicators,


provenance metadata.


User learns:


structural quality and confidence.



Step 2 — Interaction analysis
System computes:


hydrogen bonds,


packing contacts,


aromatic clusters,


salt bridges,


interaction network topology.


User sees:


interaction overlays,


residue-centric interaction summaries,


network connectivity.


User learns:


what stabilizes the structure,


which residues are structurally important.



Step 3 — Residue inspection
User selects residue.
System displays:


local interaction environment,


energetic compatibility context,


network role,


burial/packing information.


User learns:


why the residue matters structurally.



Step 4 — Mutation introduction
User specifies mutation.
System:


applies substitution,


recomputes local interactions,


recalculates compatibility and network perturbation.



Step 5 — WT vs mutant comparison
User sees:


lost interactions,


gained clashes,


altered packing,


centrality shifts,


compatibility perturbation maps.


User learns:


how the mutation perturbs structural constraints.



Step 6 — Mechanistic interpretation
System generates:


mechanistic explanations,


structural adaptation hypotheses,


destabilization signals.


Interpretation examples:


“mutation introduces steric pressure”


“aromatic packing network weakened”


“local helix compatibility reduced”



Step 7 — Severity assessment
System provides:


low/moderate/high structural disruption likelihood.


Important:
Severity remains:


structural,


mechanistic,


non-clinical.


User learns:


how concerning the mutation may be structurally.



Step 8 — Confidence interpretation
System communicates:


confidence levels,


structural uncertainty,


inference depth,


speculative vs observed conclusions.


User learns:


how much to trust each conclusion.

7. Success criteria / signal for graduation
Success signals
The project is successful if users:


generate mechanistic mutation hypotheses faster,


rely on interaction explanations during analysis,


use outputs for experimental planning,


export figures/explanations for papers or presentations,


reduce manual PyMOL inspection effort.


Strong signals:


repeat use by structural biologists,


use in mutagenesis prioritization,


educational adoption,


high engagement with interaction reasoning views.



Scientific validation signals
Success indicators:


mechanistic interpretations correlate with known structural effects,


interaction perturbations align with experimental mutation outcomes,


expert users find explanations chemically reasonable.



Product scalability signals
Scale investment justified if:


users trust explanation layers,


interaction-network views prove uniquely useful,


mutation reasoning differentiates product from standard viewers.



Assumptions requiring early validation
Most important assumptions:


Users value mechanistic explanation more than raw prediction.


Interaction-network perturbations correlate with structural sensitivity.


Users trust layered confidence explanations.


Constraint-network reasoning is interpretable and actionable.



Failure signals
The idea should be reconsidered if:


users overtrust speculative outputs,


explanations feel decorative rather than actionable,


network scores lack biological meaning,


mutation interpretations contradict established structural reasoning,


interaction visualizations become cognitively overwhelming,


users only use the system as a structure viewer.



Graduation criteria for broader operationalization
The project is ready for broader productionization if:


interaction reasoning proves reproducibly useful,


confidence handling prevents major overinterpretation,


structural interpretations remain scientifically defensible,


mutation severity rankings correlate meaningfully with known destabilization trends,


expert users consistently understand and trust the explanation hierarchy.
